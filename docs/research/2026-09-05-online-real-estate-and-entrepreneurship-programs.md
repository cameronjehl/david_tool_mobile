## 3. Top 3 online real estate programs

### 1. Wharton Online + Wall Street Prep: Real Estate Investing & Analysis Certificate

- **Facts.** $4,800 with early registration, otherwise $4,800 plus a $200 application fee (five monthly payments of $960 available). 8 weeks at about 10 hours a week. Self-paced modules with weekly live office hours. Cohorts: October 5 to December 6, 2026, then February and June 2027. No degree requirement; designed for early-career finance professionals and non-finance professionals whose roles need fluency. Faculty chair Todd Sinai with Benjamin Keys and Jessie Handbury plus practitioner instructors. Digital certificate issued by Wharton Online and 65 NASBA CPE credits.
- **Why it is first.** It is the only elite-branded, open-enrollment real estate credential that teaches institutional underwriting hands-on in Excel with a capstone. That is the skill that lets you read a deal the way David does, and it is what acquisitions, brokerage, and development shops actually test for. The Wharton name needs no explanation to your parents or to anyone in David's network.
- **Watch-outs.** Ten hours a week is real. Skip the October 5 cohort (it is your move-in month and the 90-day review window) and take February or June 2027. The certificate is issued by Wharton Online, not the Wharton degree program; word it that way.

### 2. Cornell eCornell: Commercial Real Estate Certificate

- **Facts.** $3,900 (6 monthly payments of $700, or 3 of $1,350; a 30% code was live through September 8, 2026). 3 months, 6 sequential 2-week courses, 3 to 5 hours a week. New cohorts roughly monthly from September 16, 2026 through March 2027. No degree requirement; Excel familiarity recommended. Nolan School faculty (Brad Wellstead, Jeanne Varney, Jan deRoos). Certificate from the Cornell SC Johnson College of Business plus 60 professional development hours.
- **Why it is second.** Ivy brand, the lightest weekly load of any elite option, and the widest survey of commercial real estate (leasing, valuation, development, finance, property management). It is the pick if 10 hours a week is unrealistic in 2027.
- **Watch-outs.** Less quantitative depth than Wharton/WSP; practitioners describe eCornell certificates as résumé signals rather than hiring differentiators.

### 3. University of Alabama (Culverhouse): online BS in Real Estate

- **Facts.** Fully online, 120 credits, $399 per credit hour plus a $15 per credit college fee (about $49,700 for the full degree; part-time pacing allowed). Tracks in Real Estate Finance & Investments and Real Estate Brokerage & Management. AACSB-accredited business school; the university is SACSCOC-accredited. First-time freshmen apply with a high-school transcript; ACT/SAT not required through the Fall 2026 entry term, and for 2027 entry only if the high-school GPA is below 3.0 (the second point is from the research agent's reading of UA's freshman page; confirm). Applications accepted year-round.
- **Why it is third.** It is the only fully online real estate bachelor's from a US flagship with an AACSB business school. If your parents want the degree itself to be in real estate, this is the one that a Miami employer and a Memphis lawyer both recognize.
- **Watch-outs.** It is a conventional 120-credit degree with general-education courses, semester pacing, and no residency discount. The RICS-accredited alternative is the University of the Built Environment's BSc (Hons) Real Estate Management (£28,566, 4.5 years part-time at 15 to 20 hours a week), but its admissibility for a US diploma is unconfirmed and its brand is unknown in Miami.

### The foundation under all three: the Florida sales associate license

Not a "program," but the credential Miami's industry legally requires and the cheapest licensed floor available to you.

- **Requirements (Florida Statute 475.17 and the DBPR checklist).** Age 18 or older, a high-school diploma or equivalent, a Social Security number, a 63-hour FREC-approved pre-license course (online and self-paced allowed; completion valid two years), the DBPR application ($62.75 under the 2022 fee rule; older pages still show $83.75), electronic fingerprints ($50 to $80), and the state exam through Pearson VUE ($36.75 per attempt): 100 questions, 3.5 hours, 75 to pass. First-time pass rates ran about 50% in early 2025, so study to the 19-area outline. The license is issued inactive until a sponsoring broker activates it, and a 45-hour post-license course is due before the first renewal.
- **Cost and time.** Course $100 to $500 (Gold Coast Schools has Miami campuses and a livestream; Colibri, Kaplan, Aceable, and The CE Shop are cheaper online) plus about $150 in state fees: roughly $300 to $700 and 6 to 12 weeks. You can start the course now and file the application the week the diploma arrives.
- **What to do with it.** You do not need to sell houses. Held under a broker, it makes you a licensed real estate marketer in a city where Compass alone closed $3.7 billion in $500k-plus sales last year, opens MIAMI YPN and Florida Realtors YPN (free with membership), and is the second-income floor your July doc was asking for. New agents' median first-two-year income is about $8,100, so treat brokerage as an option, not a plan.
- **Add-ons that compound it.** CCIM candidate membership ($695 a year) and the CI 101 course, open to you now even though the designation needs 2 to 5 years of experience; NAIOP South Florida Developing Leaders ($300 a year, under 35, formal mentorship); ULI Southeast Florida Young Leaders; A.CRE Accelerator ($697) or Wall Street Prep REFM ($499) for modeling practice.

## 4. Top 3 online entrepreneurship programs

### 1. Harvard Business School Online: Core Business Essentials, then the Certificate of Specialization in Entrepreneurship & Innovation

- **Facts.** Core Business Essentials replaced CORe on June 17, 2026: $2,799, 17 weeks, 5 to 8 hours a week, self-paced and available any time, covering business analytics, financial accounting, business strategy, workplace dynamics, and a capstone; income-based grants exist. The Certificate of Specialization is any three courses from the Entrepreneurship & Innovation track within 18 months at $1,949 each: Entrepreneurship Essentials (4 weeks), Launching Tech Ventures (5 weeks), and Entrepreneurial Marketing (4 weeks) are the three that map to your work. Eligibility is age 18 and English proficiency; enrollment is a short form, not an application. Total for the full stack: about $8,650 over roughly 30 weeks.
- **Why it is first.** It is the single most recognizable brand available with no prerequisites, it is graded and case-based, and Core teaches the accounting, analytics, and strategy that are the language of an M&A shop. It makes you more useful to David within months and gives your parents a Harvard-branded, graded credential six months after the move.
- **Watch-outs.** No Harvard credit and no alumni status; on paper it is "Harvard Business School Online." Say it that way. CLIMB, HBS Online's year-long credential, requires five years of experience and is not for you yet.

### 2. Wharton Online: Entrepreneurship Certificate

- **Facts.** Launched January 21, 2026. $3,850 for three self-paced courses averaging six weeks each: Becoming Entrepreneurial (Lori Rosenkopf), From Idea to Impact (Christian Terwiesch and Karl Ulrich), and From Plan to Performance (Henning Piezunka). "No prior background in entrepreneurship is required." Digital certificate and a badge per course, plus one CEU per course.
- **Why it is second.** Top-ranked entrepreneurship faculty, the cheapest brand-name entrepreneurship certificate found, and the lightest load. It is the natural second credential once Core is done, or the first if you want the entrepreneurship label before the finance one.
- **Watch-outs.** New program with little track record; lighter than HBS Online's graded case courses.

### 3. Stanford Online: Innovation & Entrepreneurship Professional Certificate, with Y Combinator Startup School as the free founder layer

- **Facts.** Stanford Center for Professional Development courses at about $995 each; the certificate requires 8 of 12 courses (about $7,960). Self-paced, no prerequisites stated, faculty including Jennifer Aaker, Bob Sutton, and Perry Klebahn. Pricing came from the official pages' indexed text because the site blocks automated reading; confirm before paying. Y Combinator Startup School is free, self-paced (about 7 weeks at 1 to 2 hours a week), includes co-founder matching, and requires no application.
- **Why it is third.** The Stanford name and the design-thinking material are valuable, but it is the least rigorous and the priciest per hour of the three, so it is the one to drop if the budget is tight. Startup School gives you the actual founder community for nothing.
- **Watch-outs.** Stanford's serious entrepreneurship programs (Ignite, LEAD) require a bachelor's or mid-career experience. Babson, the name most people expect here, has no online undergraduate program and its Miami campus is graduate-only; its $720 on-demand certificate is a nine-hour badge, fine as an add-on, not a credential of substance.

### Founder money and "skip college" programs, for completeness

Thiel Fellowship: $250,000 over two years, no equity, age 22 or under, no degree, rolling applications; accepting it means not enrolling in a degree. 1517 Fund: grants from $1,000 and pre-seed checks for founders without degrees. Z Fellows: one week, mostly virtual, optional $10,000, job-compatible. Praxis still operates (8 months, $7,500, job-offer guarantee) but places people into jobs, which you already have. Founder Institute charges $1,199 to $1,649 plus 2.5% equity, needs 20 hours a week, and graduates fewer than 30% of those admitted; skip it. UnCollege/Year On is defunct.

And UF Online's non-resident rate is already less than half of on-campus non-resident tuition, so it is the cheapest state-university bachelor's even before residency.

**The residency rule, which decides the price.** Florida Statute 1009.21 requires 12 consecutive months of bona fide Florida residence before the first day of the term. A student under 24 is classified on the parents' residency unless independent under Board of Governors Regulation 7.005, and two of the independence tests are within your control: you are "not eligible to be claimed as a dependent by his or her parent... for federal income tax purposes," or you pay more than half of your own non-resident tuition. Practical sequence: get a Florida driver's license, voter registration, a lease in your name, and a declaration of domicile in the first week of October 2026; ask your parents' CPA whether they should stop claiming you for tax year 2026; enroll no earlier than a term starting on or after October 1, 2027, which means Spring 2028 at FIU or UF. Every document must be dated at least 12 months before that term.
